How to Calculate Cubic Yards (The Master Formula)

A cubic yard is a unit of volume equal to a cube with dimensions of 3 feet in length, 3 feet in width, and 3 feet in depth (3' × 3' × 3' = 27 cubic feet). To convert any area into cubic yards, multiply length by width to find total square feet, multiply by depth (in feet), and divide by 27.

Official Civil Engineering Formula
Cubic Yards = (Length [ft] × Width [ft] × (Depth [inches] ÷ 12)) ÷ 27

Worked Example: To fill a raised garden bed or gravel patio that measures 20 feet long by 10 feet wide with a depth of 4 inches:

  1. Calculate square footage: 20 ft × 10 ft = 200 sq. ft.
  2. Convert depth to feet: 4 inches ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet
  3. Calculate total cubic feet: 200 sq. ft. × 0.333 ft = 66.67 cu. ft.
  4. Divide by 27 to find cubic yards: 66.67 ÷ 27 = 2.47 cubic yards
  5. Add 10% contingency for compaction: 2.47 × 1.10 = 2.72 cubic yards

Frequently Asked Questions About Cubic Yards

The standard formula for cubic yards is: Cubic Yards = (Length in feet × Width in feet × Depth in feet) ÷ 27. If depth is in inches, divide inches by 12 first: Cubic Yards = (Length ft × Width ft × (Depth in ÷ 12)) ÷ 27.

A cubic yard is a cube that measures 3 feet long by 3 feet wide by 3 feet deep. Multiplying 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft equals 27 cubic feet. Therefore, dividing total cubic feet by 27 converts the volume directly into cubic yards.

One cubic yard covers 324 sq ft at 1 inch depth, 162 sq ft at 2 inches depth, 108 sq ft at 3 inches depth, 81 sq ft at 4 inches depth, 54 sq ft at 6 inches depth, and 27 sq ft at 12 inches depth.

One cubic yard of crushed gravel weighs approximately 2,800 lbs (1.4 US tons). Screened topsoil weighs between 2,200 and 2,600 lbs (1.1 to 1.3 tons) depending on moisture, while dry mulch weighs about 800 to 1,000 lbs (0.45 tons).

Contractors recommend ordering 5% to 10% extra material for loose landscaping aggregates like mulch, topsoil, and gravel to account for ground settling and uneven subgrades. For poured concrete slabs, add 10% for spillage and form deflection.
